The actual signed log-coordinate measure has finite total variation: a_n decays as n^(-3/2) in the upper tail. Its moving-interval convolution is both L1 and bounded at fixed x,T, hence L2. The Fourier transform of the interval of log length 1/T has squared magnitude T^(-2) sinc²(t/(2T)). Thus Plancherel gives exactly 2pi*T²*J. Evenness of |P|² and the positive lower bound c*=sinc²(1/2) on [-T,T] supply the factor pi*T/(c*x*log T). This is a valid majorant for the whole centered signal, not an entrywise comparison of signed arithmetic kernels.

The source prime-count cap, multiplied by log(u+h), has leading coefficient 2alpha/(alpha-1). In particular its central coefficient is four, not two. Counting every integer base for higher powers gives the stated negligible relative error on the large-u ranges where the cap is used. The global log-Lipschitz constant 3/2 holds across the kink u=x. None of these steps omits prime powers or uses a false short-interval asymptotic.

I checked the exact center and cross moment separately. The continuous moment integral u*a_u² is 4x²/3. Fubini makes the mixed term T^(-2) times sum Lambda(n)*n*a_n² up to relative O(1/T). Splitting the weight at x gives n²/x below x and x³/n² above it. Partial summation with the ordinary RH error yields O(x^(3/2)log²x); the common endpoint cancels when the split is recombined. This is a global weighted estimate, so no short-interval RH estimate is being assumed. Its precision is only relative to x²/T², as the author states.

The local cap is extended to all u by an explicit cutoff Q=T^(5/4). Below Q, the crude integer count yields exactly Q³/T²+Q²/T+Q before dividing by x and including log²Q. Relative to x²/T², the largest values at alpha=7/4 have exponents -3/2, -7/4 and -2. No prime lies below 2*exp(-1/T). Above Q, the prime cap ratio is at most 10+o(1), and the higher-power ratio tends uniformly to zero; eleven is a legitimate eventual bound. On the remote tails, Fubini enlarges the same weighted prime moment by only an exp(O(1/T)) boundary factor. The lower and upper tails then have relative bounds exp(-3R) and exp(-R), respectively, from psi(y)<<y. First T increases and then R increases. This proves the claimed uniform capped second-moment bound across the fixed alpha interval.

Substitution into J=integral A²-2 integral Am+integral m² correctly gives (4/3)(c-1), so the negative mixed term contributes the full minus two. At alpha=2, J<= (4+o(1))*x²/T². The final pointwise upper estimate is therefore (4pi/c*+o(1))*T/log T, which is much weaker than the existing actual form-factor estimate and the R16 constant bump bound. Integrating the available positive majorant does not produce a strict constant deficit. Its divergent right side is a defect of this estimate, not growth of the true Bragg statistic.

The fixed-cap-times-first-moment step loses the needed fluctuation scale even with centering retained. A different fixed cap greater than one leaves the same power loss. The stated O(T log T/x) cap-excess criterion is restricted to this particular bounding step and is not a universal requirement for a successful method. Joint covariance or oscillatory sieve information is not ruled out.
