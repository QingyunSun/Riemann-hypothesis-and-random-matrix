## Independent real-line test of the eleven-root survival vector

Let $p_s(z)=\sum a_j e^{s j(N-j)} z^j$. Centering its trigonometric polynomial multiplies the $j$-th term by the frequency phase $j-N/2$. Up to the positive scalar $e^{sN^2/4}$, ordinary heat at elapsed time $s$ multiplies this term by $e^{-s(j-N/2)^2}$. In particular $e^s=85/84$ has the sign for the collision direction. The endpoint coefficients do not change, and the pair of coefficients at indices $j,N-j$ acquire the same real factor. The polynomial therefore remains self-inversive, with its roots occurring in reciprocal-conjugate pairs.

Writing the extended Newton sums as $p_{-k}=\bar p_k$ is valid for all configurations because the root multiset is invariant under $z\mapsto 1/\bar z$. The Hermitian Laurent moment matrix is $VJV^*$, where $J$ pairs reciprocal-conjugate columns by a permutation and fixes unit-circle columns. For distinct roots, Sylvester inertia shows that each off-circle pair contributes one negative square, and that all roots on the circle give positive definiteness. Multiplicities make $V$ rank-deficient. A strictly positive matrix thus gives exactly $N$ distinct unit roots; a strictly negative quadratic direction rules out distinct unit roots.

Endpoint survival yields path survival. Centered real simple roots continued backwards from that endpoint obey the repulsive angular equation. The derivative of $\log|\Delta|$ in this direction is a positive multiple of its squared gradient. Since the angles lie in a compact configuration space and $|\Delta|$ is bounded above, nondecrease from a positive initial value excludes collision; it also bounds each individual pair distance below on every backward interval. Uniqueness of polynomial evolution identifies that reverse trajectory with the original flow back to zero. Starting with distinct unit roots, departure from the circle first requires a multiple root by the reciprocal-conjugate symmetry and local uniqueness of isolated simple roots.

### Independent Cayley–Sturm classifier

The following code replaces all Newton sums, all Toeplitz matrices, and the LDL test. It applies the Cayley map $z=z_0(t+i)/(t-i)$, with $z_0=(7+24i)/25$ on the circle, normalizes the transformed polynomial by its nonzero leading coefficient, and obtains a real monic polynomial. Self-inversiveness makes all of its coefficients real exactly. The checker verifies that each imaginary enclosure contains zero. Positive leading coefficients of consecutive negative polynomial remainders, with degrees dropping by one, imply $N$ simple real roots by Sturm's theorem at the two infinities. Conversely, if all $N$ roots are real simple, strict interlacing and the signs beyond the extreme roots force each next negative remainder to have its expected degree and positive leading coefficient. A strictly negative leading coefficient therefore rejects survival. An unresolved sign raises an exception. The [portable replay](check_sturm.py) independently enumerates anchored eleven-subsets of 22 sites and selects each dihedral orbit by lexicographically minimizing its circular gap necklace, with reversed orientations included. It computes all reciprocal complements, selects those with matching sorted gaps, and checks their complete ordered pair-displacement counts. Only the Python standard library and python-flint are required.

At 256-bit working precision with python-flint 0.9.0 the independent Sturm test returned 324 and 443 survivors in the first and second columns. The difference counts for $-1,0,1$ were $137,1096,256$. Every sign used in the replay was strict. The SHA-256 fingerprint across every paired input and both indicators is `7f58227e2708a2d288b35317932ae2d1fa57947e36b737c13d4355ec681ee2f8`. It agrees with the independently enumerated Toeplitz classifications on all 2,978 inputs. The byte stream consists of 1,489 compact ASCII JSON lines `[A,B,survives_A,survives_B]`, each terminated by LF, sorted lexicographically by the canonical integer site tuples. Its length is 94,065 bytes. These checks verify the survival vector separately from the moment-matrix rank argument.
