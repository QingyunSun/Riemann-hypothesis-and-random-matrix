### Exact resolution of the 399-versus-403 ambiguity

The original tomography script explicitly rotates configurations into orbits and uses an SVD tolerance to calculate the constraint rank. The short brief drops the orbit qualification. These changes matter even if every calculation is done exactly. For balanced equations, the within-orbit distributions are free. [Tomography source](https://github.com/galpha-ai/Alpha-devbox/blob/0edc027d25b604c97588f7432ee5bac51452b912/research/riemann-rmt/riemann-impostors/counterexamples/fibre_tomography.py).

**Rank formula.** Put $\rho_j=N-j$, $1\le j\le N$. Let $\nu$ run through decreasing length-$N$ integer vectors with $\sum\nu_j=0$, $\sum\max(\nu_j,0)\le N$. Count the distinct sets
$$E_\nu=\{\nu_j+\rho_j\bmod 2N:1\le j\le N\}$$
having $N$ distinct entries. This count is the rank of the balanced-moment system including normalization. The rotation-invariant affine dimension equals the number of rotation orbits minus this rank. For measures with arbitrary labeled-site probabilities, the starting dimension before subtracting rank is $\binom{2N}{N}$. ACUE has strictly positive weight on every subset, so imposing probability nonnegativity leaves the affine dimension unchanged.

**Proof.** The degree-$d$ power-sum monomials span the degree-$d$ symmetric polynomials in characteristic zero. Products with their negative-degree counterparts, summed over $d\le N$, span all Laurent monomial symmetric polynomials $m_\nu$ in the stated range. Indeed, multiply the monomial symmetric functions of the positive and negative parts. Disjoint placements give the desired $m_\nu$ with coefficient one. Every overlapping placement cancels at least one unit of positive mass and has already been generated by induction. The rational Schur characters indexed by the same weights are another basis. Adding a large common integer to each weight reduces triangularity to the usual Schur-to-monomial expansion, and dominance can only lower the largest partial sum, which here is the positive mass. Multiplying by the Vandermonde, nonzero on every sample, turns each character into an alternating Fourier determinant with exponent rows $\nu+\rho$. Repeated residues vanish; matching exponent sets give proportional rows up to sign. Distinct residue sets give linearly independent rows because the exterior power of the invertible $2N$-point discrete Fourier transform is invertible. Complexified rank equals the real rank since the constraints include each observable and its conjugate. Rotation-invariant rows have the same rank on orbit representatives.

A short replay of the rank formula uses integer partitions only.

```python
def parts(n, minimum=1):
    if n == 0:
        yield ()
    for j in range(minimum, n + 1):
        for rest in parts(n-j, j):
            yield (j,) + rest

def balanced_rank(N):
    residues = set()
    for d in range(N+1):
        partitions = list(parts(d))
        for p in partitions:
            for q in partitions:
                if len(p)+len(q) > N:
                    continue
                nu = p[::-1] + (0,)*(N-len(p)-len(q)) + tuple(-v for v in q)
                E = tuple(sorted((a+N-1-i) % (2*N) for i,a in enumerate(nu)))
                if len(set(E)) == N:
                    residues.add(E)
    return len(residues)
```

For the separate midpoint test, let $d$ be the gap, $m$ its midpoint, and $h=d/2$. If the zeros within ordinate distance $H>1/2$ of $m$ are known to lie on the line, a remote off-line pair at real offsets $\pm u$ and vertical offset $v$ has average real log-derivative contribution $h(A-u^2)/[(A+u^2)^2-4h^2u^2]$, where $A=h^2+v^2$. Since $u^2/A\le\epsilon=1/(4H^2)<1$, the ratio to the projected Poisson term $h/A$ is at least $c=(1-\epsilon)/(1+\epsilon)^2>0$. The positive denominator is a product of the two squared distances. All on-line terms have ratio one. Subtract the two selected simple roots' exact contribution 4 to $2d\Re(\xi'/\xi)$ before dividing the residual by $c$. This bounds the pair energy computed from projected ordinates. Interpreting it as a classical Lehmer-pair certificate still requires the corresponding hypotheses on actual zeros.
